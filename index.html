One terminal for all your computers.

Run commands and long jobs on your other devices, move files between them, see how they are doing, and see who, or which AI agent, is working where.

Latest: Kache 0.9.0, an early release, out 29 October 2026

Download

Windows

Windows 10 and 11, x64. In PowerShell:

irm https://simonmalm.github.io/kache/install.ps1 | iex

Or download the installer (MSI, 8.6 MB) and open it. Until releases are signed, Windows may call it an unrecognised app: choose More info, then Run anyway.SHA-256 cee5a5a5d088c8d5741c702cbabb75f306ee9197d6fc205624328a159fc6708e

Linux

x86_64 desktops.

The Linux package is not in this release yet; it comes with a later one.

Each command downloads the installer, checks its SHA-256 and installs Kache for you alone: no administrator rights, nothing else to install first. Kache then starts at login; its owl sits among the tray icons. Pair a second device with kache pair ADDRESS.

What it does

  • Commands and jobs anywhere. Run a command on any device and watch its output live, or start a long job that keeps running after you disconnect.
  • Files between devices. Push, pull, copy and sync folders; every change can be undone.
  • Health at a glance. CPU, memory, disks and GPUs of every device, with a week of history.
  • Built for AI agents. Claude Code, Codex and others use Kache through its CLI or MCP, labelled by name, with a chat to talk to you and each other.
  • Relays. Chains of steps across devices, agents and people that Kache carries through by itself.
  • You stay in charge. Lock a device or its GPU for agents, set CPU, memory and temperature limits, and let Kache put work on whichever machine is free.
  • Private by design. Each device has its own key; nothing goes through anyone else's server. Away from home, use it over Tailscale.
  • Small and quiet. Two native programs, little memory, almost no CPU while idle.

What's in 0.9.0

The first public release: an early one, with rough edges, for Windows 10 and 11 (x64). A Linux package follows.

  • Commands and long jobs on any of your devices, with live output, a log for every job and evidence when something fails.
  • Files between devices: push, pull, copy, sync and undo; read and patch files without a shell.
  • Health of every device (CPU, memory, disks, GPUs) with a week of history.
  • People and AI agents work side by side: everything shows who did it, a chat with mentions, read receipts and urgent messages, warnings before work collides, and gaze (an agent sees and uses another screen, with your permission).
  • Relays: chains of steps across devices, agents and people that Kache carries through by itself.
  • Locks and limits for agents (a device or its GPU, CPU, memory and temperature), and any as the device to put work where there is room.
  • Data tools that work the same in every shell: convert, query, render, hash, archive and extract.
  • kache mcp for AI agents, and kache guide for everything they use.
  • The window: terminal, chat, jobs, relays, devices and people, with the owl in the tray.
